- The two OpenAI adapters read a prompt-cache write into `usage.cacheCreationTokens`: `OpenAIResponsesAPIAdapter` from `usage.input_tokens_details.cache_write_tokens`, `OpenAIAdapter` (Chat Completions) from `usage.prompt_tokens_details.cache_write_tokens`. Under the cache-inclusive normalization the written tokens leave `inputTokens` and are priced on their own: every built-in OpenAI row carries `cacheWritePerMillion` — 1.25× input from GPT-5.6 on, the guide's 30-minute rate, and the input rate on earlier models and on `gpt-6-astra-law`, whose page rows show no write price. The Responses API's 12-hour writes (`cache_write_tokens_details.ttl_12h_tokens`) ride the long-TTL slot, `cacheCreation1hTokens`, and the four GPT-5.6/GPT-6 rows price them at 2.5× input (`cacheWrite1hPerMillion`); Chat Completions reports no TTL split, so its writes are all priced at the 30-minute rate. `estimatedCost.cacheWrite` is now a number (0 when nothing was written) for every call through either adapter priced by a built-in row, where it was undefined. Before, the field was not read, a write was counted and priced as ordinary input, and the GPT-5.6+ premium was missing from `estimatedCost`.
- A GPT-5.6 id the table has no row for (`gpt-5.6` itself, or a variant) is unpriced and warns, rather than priced by the `gpt-5` row at a tenth of its rates.

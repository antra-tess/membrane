- The Anthropic cache keepalive honours a provider's stated wait. It replays recorded requests on its own timer, so a caller that holds its own calls to a model for a `retry-after` (agent-framework's provider waits) could not stop a poke to that model inside the wait. Now, when a call to a model through the same adapter states a wait (a foreground `complete`/`stream` failure carrying `retryAfterMs`, or a refused poke), no lineage of that model is poked until the wait passes. That covers lineages other than the one that failed, including one already due in the same tick. The hold lives in that adapter's keepalive, so another adapter instance's lineages of the model aren't held.
  - Holds reduce by maximum: a later, shorter wait never shortens one. A wait of 0, or a value that isn't a positive number, holds nothing.
  - A wait that cannot be held as an instant (not finite, or past the last instant a Date can represent) holds until the keepalive stops; that is its whole lifecycle, since the keepalive is an optional optimisation. Only the passing of a wait ends a hold. A caller's release of its own admission, such as agent-framework's agent-scoped `release-provider-wait`, reopens that caller's foreground calls but does not clear the model-wide background hold.
  - A failed poke that the adapter classifies as retryable (a rate limit, an overload, any 5xx, a timeout or a connection failure) is paced and never counts toward `maxConsecutiveErrors`; the count stays where it was and is not reset. Every lineage of the model is held until the later of the provider's stated wait (when it gave one a hold accepts) and the model's own backoff, which doubles with each such refusal in a run, from one `checkIntervalMs` up to `refreshAfterMs`. A run ends when a poke is accepted, or when a refusal comes more than two check intervals after the end of the run's last hold, so a real call to the model mid-storm doesn't restart the backoff of its other lineages. A paced refusal doesn't age its lineage, so once the hold passes the lineage is poked again while its entry may still be warm. So an overload storm whose 529s state no wait, or an outage, backs the keepalive off, and pokes resume after it; a poke refused as retryable is never repeated unpaced. A failure that is not retryable, or that nothing classifies, still counts toward the breaker, whatever wait it states, and the breaker is what bounds a run of those; a wait it states still holds the model. Before, a refused poke was one error toward a breaker whose `stop()` is permanent, so three rate-limit episodes, or one overload storm with several lineages of a model due together, could disable the keepalive for the adapter's life.
  - Every hold, from a stated wait or from the backoff, is reported by a new `held` keepalive event (`{ type: 'held', model, until, reason }`, `until` null for a hold until stop) when it is set. `CacheKeepalive` takes an optional third constructor argument that reads a failure's stated wait and retryability. The Anthropic adapter passes its own error classification; poke receipts carry the error its send threw.
  - A `CacheKeepalive` option given as `undefined` takes its default, as one left out does, so `enabled: undefined` is enabled, as the adapter already read it.
  - A poke no longer drops a lineage that a real call recorded under the same key while the poke was in flight.
